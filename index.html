Speaking Module — Part 2, Cue Cards

Stop hunting for ideas.
Build a deck you can reuse.

Nine or ten prepared idea-banks — a family member, your best friend, your school, one good trip — cover the overwhelming majority of cue cards examiners actually hand out. This is the first module of a personal IELTS notebook, built to actually be used, not skimmed once.

01 · Overview

What Part 2 is actually testing

1:00
Prep, with a pencil
You get a card, paper, and 60 seconds. This is note-jotting time, not idea-inventing time — the ideas need to already exist in your head.
2:00
Speak, unscripted
The examiner won't interrupt before 2 minutes. Silence, restarts, and "um, I don't know" cost you far more than an imperfect story.
4
Criteria scored
Fluency & coherence, lexical resource, grammar, pronunciation — none of them reward truthfulness. A well-told invented story scores exactly the same as a true one.
"

Memorized answers fail because cue cards are randomized combinations, not fixed topics. You can't pre-write "describe a helpful person" and expect it to survive being handed "describe someone who taught you something" instead. What survives is a small set of flexible ideas — a specific person, place, or event you know well — that you can angle toward whatever the card actually asks.

02 · Method

The reusable idea-generation framework

The video's actual instruction is simple: fix one real idea per category, then write down its good qualities, its flaws, and one extraordinary detail, so the idea is ready before you see a card. The six lenses below are a structured way to run that same prep — turning "good / bad / extraordinary" notes into the concrete who/what/when/where/why/feeling material a full two-minute answer needs.
