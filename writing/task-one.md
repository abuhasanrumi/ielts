# 📚 IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 — MASTER NOTES
*Band 9 Complete Reference · Based on IELTS-UP*

---

## ⚡ THE UNIVERSAL STRUCTURE (for ALL task types)

```
Task 1 Structure
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│   Introduction (1–2 sentences)  │
│   paraphrase the question card  │
└─────────────────────────────────┘

┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│      Overview (1 paragraph)     │
│       general trends only,      │
│       start with "Overall,"     │
│─────────────────────────────────│  
│     Feature (2–3 paragraphs)    │  ← Body
│   numbers, dates, comparisons   │
│─────────────────────────────────│
│ ❌ NO CONCLUSION EVER in Task 1 │
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

> Minimum 150 words · Spend ~20 minutes · Write in **past simple** tense (for past data)

## PARAGRAPH 1 — INTRODUCTION

**Formula:** `[Graph type] + [verb] + [what it shows] + [time period] + [countries/names if applicable]`

### Step 1 — NEVER change the graph type name
| You see | You write |
|---|---|
| bar chart | bar chart |
| line graph | line graph |
| pie chart | pie chart |
| table | table |
| diagram | diagram |
| map | map / pictures |

### Step 2 — Replace the verb (ALWAYS paraphrase it)
| Original | Replacements |
|---|---|
| shows | illustrates · represents · depicts · provides information about/on · compares · describes · draws the conclusion of · explains why |

**Special uses:**
- Use **compares** only when several items are compared
  - *"compares X in terms of Y"* → The table compares five companies in terms of employees.
  - *"compares X in year1 and year2"* → The graph compares population in 2000 and 2013.
- Use **explains why / gives reason why** only if the graph explains something
- Use **draws the conclusion of (a survey)** for survey-based charts

### Step 3 — Paraphrase the topic content

**Technique A — Use synonyms:**
| Original word | Synonym(s) |
|---|---|
| number of | quantity of *(not "amount of" for countable things)* |
| spending | expenditure |
| rate | percentage |
| ratio | proportion |
| information | data |
| change | increase / decrease / variation |
| share | portion |
| place | site |

**Technique B — Use the word "how":**
- "shows the number of people" → "shows **how many** people"
- "depicts changes in spending on" → "depicts **how much** spending changed on"
- "percentages of industry sectors contribution" → "**how** different sectors contributed … percentage-wise"

*(Find the action noun → turn it into a verb → rebuild the sentence)*

**Technique C — Add specifics from the graph:**
- Instead of "three countries" → name them: "the UK, France, and Germany"
- Instead of "retail sectors" → list them: "electronics, food and beverages, home furnishings, and video games"
- Add the word "different" or "regional" for extra detail

### Step 4 — Paraphrase the time period
| Original | Paraphrase options |
|---|---|
| between 2010 and 2020 | from 2010 to 2020 |
| from 2010 to 2020 | between 2010 and 2020 |
| in 2010 | in the year 2010 |
| in 2010 and 2020 | in the years 2010 and 2020 · in 2010 and 2020 respectively |

> ❌ Skip the word **"below"** — it's just for layout and the examiner won't see the chart below your writing.

### ✅ Introduction Examples

**Bar chart:**
> Q: *"The graphs give information about computer ownership as a percentage of the population between 2002 and 2010, and by level of education for the years 2002 and 2010."*
> A: *"The bar charts show data about computer ownership, with a further classification by level of education, from 2002 to 2010."*

**Pie chart:**
> Q: *"The two pie charts below show the online shopping sales for retail sectors in Canada in 2007 and 2009."*
> A: *"The two pie charts compare the percentages of online shopping sales in Canada for different retail sectors (electronics, food and beverages, home furnishings, and video games) in the years 2007 and 2009."*

**Line graph:**
> Q: *"The line graph below shows the household recycling rates in three different countries between 2005 and 2015."*
> A: *"The line graph illustrates the regional household recycling rates in the UK, France and Germany from 2005 to 2015."*

**Pie chart (Turkey):**
> Q: *"The two charts below show the percentages of industry sectors' contribution to the economy of Turkey in 2000 and 2016."*
> A: *"The two pie charts illustrate how different industry sectors contributed to the economy of Turkey percentage-wise in the years 2000 and 2016."*

**Diagram:**
> Q: *"The diagram illustrates how steel roads are manufactured in the furniture industry."*
> A: *"The diagram explains the way in which steel roads are produced for the furniture industry."*

## PARAGRAPH 2 — OVERVIEW

**Always start with: `Overall,`**

Rules:
- ❌ NO numbers, percentages, or specific dates
- ✅ 2–4 key general trends only
- ✅ 1–3 sentences max (keep it short)

### How to find key features:
- **Line/bar graphs:** Which went up? Which went down? Any fluctuations? Who was highest/lowest at start vs end? Did any lines cross?
- **Pie charts:** What was the biggest slice? Smallest slice? What changed most between two time periods?
- **Diagrams/Process:** How many stages? Where does it begin and end? Any loops or cycles?
- **Maps:** What disappeared? What was added? What stayed the same?

### Overview formulas by type:

**For trends (line/bar):**
> *"Overall, [X] showed an upward trend, while [Y] experienced a downward trend throughout the period. Both [countries/items] showed some fluctuations. Although [X] initially had a lower rate, it outraced [Y] at the end of the period."*

**For pie charts:**
> *"Overall, at the beginning of the period, [X] contributed the least / was the most significant sector, while [Y] made the highest contribution. In comparison, at the end of the period, [Z] became the largest segment and the lowest contribution was made by [W]."*

**For process diagrams:**
> *"Overall, the process consists of [N] stages, beginning with [first stage] and ending up with [last stage]."*

**For maps/diagrams:**
> *"Overall, [X and Y] disappeared from the map, [Z] was reduced, and [new features] were added."*

### Useful overview vocabulary:
| Concept | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Largest | contributed the most · made the highest contribution · made up the biggest part · was the most significant · was the dominant |
| Smallest | contributed the least · made the lowest contribution · made up the smallest part · was the least significant |
| Going up | showed an upward trend · experienced a rise · increased steadily · grew throughout |
| Going down | showed a downward trend · experienced a decline · fell throughout |
| No change | remained constant · stayed the same · was unchanged |
| Contrast | while · whereas · in contrast · in comparison · although · however |
| Both/All | both countries · all three sectors · similarly |

## PARAGRAPHS 3–4 — SPECIFIC DETAILS

Always include:
- Exact numbers/percentages (use **about / around / approximately / nearly / almost** when not exact)
- Start and end values
- Notable peaks and troughs
- Comparisons between items

### How to group data (choose ONE logical method):
- **By country/item** → describe each one in its own paragraph
- **By trend** → group rising items together, falling items together *(preferred when following the overview)*
- **By time period** → describe each period in sequence
- **For process diagrams** → describe stages sequentially (never out of order)

### Expressing percentages — variety of phrases:
| Meaning | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Stating a % | accounted for X% · was X% · comprised X% · made up X% · economic output was X% · stood at X% · was at the level of X% |
| "About X%" | about · around · approximately · nearly · almost · in the region of · roughly |
| Fractions | almost a quarter (≈25%) · more than a third (>33%) · nearly a half (≈50%) · around two thirds (≈67%) · more than three quarters (>75%) · one tenth (10%) |
| Biggest/smallest | the highest · the dominant · the peak · a high point of · the most popular |
| Lowest | the lowest · the least popular · a low point of · hit a low point of about X% |

### Describing a % that can't be read exactly:
> ❌ "Sweden's divorce rate was 45% in 2011"
> ✅ "Sweden's divorce rate was **about** 45% in 2011"
> ✅ "the rate was **almost** 50%" (when it's very close but didn't reach)
> ✅ "the rate was **just over** 500" (slightly above)

## 📈 VOCABULARY TO DESCRIBE CHANGES

### Increase verbs:
| Mild | Strong | Very strong |
|---|---|---|
| rise · increase · grow · climb | surge · leap | rocket · soar · skyrocket |

### Decrease verbs:
| Mild | Strong | Very strong |
|---|---|---|
| fall · decrease · decline · dip | dive · drop | plummet · plunge |

### No change verbs:
- remained constant · stayed the same · remained steady · was unchanged · held at

### Fluctuation verbs:
- fluctuate · vary · showed fluctuations

### Change NOUNS (use with articles: "a rise OF", "a fall IN"):
| Increase | Decrease | Fluctuation |
|---|---|---|
| a rise (of) | a fall (in) | a fluctuation (of) |
| an increase (of) | a decrease (in) | a variation (in) |
| a growth (of) | a decline (in) | |
| a surge (of) | a dip (in) | |
| a peak (of) | | |

### Speed/size ADVERBS (how fast/big the change was):
| Large change | Small change |
|---|---|
| sharply · suddenly · rapidly · dramatically · significantly · considerably · markedly · steeply · substantially · spectacularly | slightly · gently · gradually · steadily · modestly · marginally · consistently |

### Speed/size ADJECTIVES (use with nouns):
| Large | Small |
|---|---|
| sharp · sudden · rapid · dramatic · steep · significant · considerable · marked · substantial · spectacular | slight · gentle · gradual · steady · consistent · modest · marginal |

### Multiplying language:
- doubled · tripled · quadrupled
- experienced a more than **three-fold** increase
- made **nearly a fourfold** jump
- rose to **one tenth** (= 10%) ← stylish way to say a percentage

## 🔗 LINKING WORDS & CONNECTORS

### Time connectors (essential for process diagrams):
| Position | Words |
|---|---|
| First stage | First of all · Firstly · To begin with · Initially |
| Next stages | After that · Then · Next · In the next stage · Following this · Subsequently · Afterwards |
| Last stage | Finally · Lastly · In the final stage |

### Contrast connectors:
- however · nevertheless · in contrast · in comparison · whereas · while · although · even though · yet · conversely · on the contrary · on the other hand

### Addition connectors:
- also · furthermore · moreover · what is more · in addition · besides · not only … but also

### Similarity connectors:
- likewise · similarly · equally · correspondingly · in the same way · both

### Result connectors:
- therefore · as a result · consequently · thus · hence

### Transition connectors:
- turning to · with regard to · as for · as far as X is concerned

## 🗺️ SPECIAL RULES BY TASK TYPE

### LINE GRAPHS & BAR CHARTS
- Group data by country OR by similar trend (trend grouping is usually better)
- Always mention values at the **start** and **end** of the period
- Note any **crossover points** (when two lines meet or one overtakes another)
- Use "hit a low/high point of" for min/max values
- Use "remained steady" for flat periods

### PIE CHARTS
- Focus on **biggest and smallest** slices in each chart
- For two pie charts: compare what **changed** and what **stayed similar**
- Creative percentage phrases: "almost a quarter" instead of "24%"
- Use "respectively" when listing two values: "8% and 5% respectively"

### PROCESS DIAGRAMS
- MUST describe stages **sequentially**
- Use "undergo" when you don't know exactly what happens: *"the material undergoes refinement"*
- Overview = number of stages + beginning + end
- No trends to discuss — just sequential description with time connectors

### MAPS / LOCATION DIAGRAMS
- Note what was **added**, **removed**, **relocated**, **expanded**, or **stayed the same**
- Give overview of overall change (e.g. "modernised," "developed," "reduced in size")
- Describe element by element in separate paragraphs

### TABLES
- Treat like bar charts — find biggest/smallest, compare rows/columns
- Don't try to mention every single number; group by notable patterns

## ⚠️ COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right |
|---|---|
| Copying the question card word for word | Paraphrase everything |
| Writing a conclusion | No conclusion in Task 1 |
| Putting numbers/dates in the overview | Overview = general trends only |
| Writing everything in one big paragraph | Use separate paragraphs for each group |
| Describing every tiny detail | Focus on key trends and patterns |
| "The amount of students" | "The **number** of students" (students are countable) |
| "In + year" vs "of + year" | ✅ "in 2010" ❌ "of 2010" |
| Exact numbers when graph is not precise | Use "about / around / approximately" |
| Writing "below" in intro (e.g. "the graph below") | Skip "below" — examiner won't see the graph below your text |
| Paraphrasing the graph TYPE | Never change: pie chart stays pie chart, diagram stays diagram |

## 🎯 QUICK-REFERENCE CHECKLIST

Before submitting, check:
- [ ] Introduction is paraphrased (not copied), includes graph type + topic + time period
- [ ] Overview starts with "Overall," — no numbers inside
- [ ] 2–4 key features in the overview
- [ ] Specific details grouped logically in 2–3 paragraphs
- [ ] Numbers supported with qualifying words (about/almost/nearly) where values are approximate
- [ ] Variety of vocabulary for increases/decreases used (not repeating same verb)
- [ ] Linking words used to connect ideas and contrast trends
- [ ] No conclusion written
- [ ] At least 150 words written
- [ ] Written in past simple tense (for historical data)

## 📝 BAND 9 MODEL ANSWERS AT A GLANCE

### Pie chart — boys' vs girls' activities
*"The two pie charts draw the conclusion of a survey of boys' and girls' cultural and leisure activities. Overall, equal quantities of both sexes enjoyed listening to music, but a dramatically larger number of girls liked reading…"*

### Line graph — phone services spending
*"The line graph illustrates the average cost that American customers spent on mobile and landline phone services annually over a 10-year period. Overall, spending on residential phone services dramatically declined, while popularity of mobile services increased sharply throughout a decade. Also, both services met at the same record in 2006…"*

### Bar chart — museum visitors
*"The bar chart compares the yearly number of visitors in 3 different museums from 2007 to 2012. Overall, the peak of visitors' number was in 2012, while the lowest point was in 2009 and 2010…"*

### Map/diagram — school changes
*"The pictures illustrate the changes which have taken place at West Park secondary school from 1950 to 2010. Firstly, significant alterations occurred to the farmland…Overall, farmland and houses disappeared from the map, playground was abridged, and there were built a science block, car park and sports field."*
