# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 — MASTER NOTES
> Band 9 Complete Reference · IELTS-UP · All chart types covered

---

## THE STRUCTURE (use this for EVERY task type)

```
  ┌──────────────────────────────────┐
  │          INTRODUCTION            │  ← 1–2 sentences · paraphrase the question
  └──────────────────────────────────┘

       ╔════════════════════════════╗
       ║          OVERVIEW          ║  ← General trends only · Start with "Overall,"
       ╠════════════════════════════╣
Body ──╢         FEATURE 1          ║  ← Specific data · numbers · comparisons
       ╠════════════════════════════╣
       ║         FEATURE 2          ║  ← More specific data
       ╚════════════════════════════╝

  ✗ NO CONCLUSION — never write one in Task 1
```

**Rules:** Minimum 150 words · ~20 minutes · Past simple tense for past data

---

## PART 1 — INTRODUCTION

**One formula works for everything:**
> `[graph type] + [paraphrased verb] + [what it shows] + [time period] + [names/countries]`

---

### Rule 1 — NEVER change the graph type name

| You see | You write |
|---|---|
| bar chart | bar chart |
| line graph | line graph |
| pie chart | pie chart |
| table | table |
| diagram | diagram |
| map | map / pictures |

---

### Rule 2 — ALWAYS replace the verb "shows"

| Use these verbs | When to use |
|---|---|
| illustrates | always fine |
| represents | always fine |
| depicts | always fine |
| provides information about/on | always fine |
| compares | only when items are compared |
| describes | always fine |
| draws the conclusion of (a survey) | for survey-based charts |
| explains why / gives reason why | only if graph explains a reason |

> **"compares" usage:**
> - *compares X **in terms of** Y* → "The table compares five cities in terms of population."
> - *compares X **in** year1 **and** year2* → "The graph compares spending in 2000 and 2010."

---

### Rule 3 — Paraphrase the topic (3 techniques)

**Technique A — Synonyms**

| Original | Replace with |
|---|---|
| number of | quantity of *(only for countable things — never "amount of")* |
| spending | expenditure |
| rate | percentage |
| ratio | proportion |
| information | data |
| change | increase / decrease / variation |
| share | portion |
| place | site |

**Technique B — Use the word "how"**

Find the action noun → turn it into a verb → rebuild with "how"

- "the percentages of industry sectors **contribution**" → "how different sectors **contributed** … percentage-wise"
- "shows the number of people" → "shows **how many** people"
- "depicts changes in spending" → "depicts **how much** spending changed"

**Technique C — Add detail from the graph itself**

- Instead of "three countries" → name them: "the UK, France and Germany"
- Instead of "retail sectors" → list them: "electronics, food and beverages, home furnishings and video games"
- Add words like "different" or "regional" for a richer sentence

---

### Rule 4 — Paraphrase the time period

| Original | Options |
|---|---|
| between 2010 and 2020 | from 2010 to 2020 |
| from 2010 to 2020 | between 2010 and 2020 |
| in 2010 | in the year 2010 |
| in 2010 and 2020 | in the years 2010 and 2020 · in 2010 and 2020 respectively |

> ✗ Skip the word **"below"** from the question (e.g. "the graph below") — the examiner won't see the chart below your text.

---

### Introduction Examples (ready to use as models)

**Bar chart:**
> *"The bar charts show data about computer ownership, with a further classification by level of education, from 2002 to 2010."*

**Pie chart:**
> *"The two pie charts compare the percentages of online shopping sales in Canada for different retail sectors (electronics, food and beverages, home furnishings and video games) in the years 2007 and 2009."*

**Line graph:**
> *"The line graph illustrates the regional household recycling rates in the UK, France and Germany from 2005 to 2015."*

**Process diagram:**
> *"The diagram explains the way in which steel roads are produced for the furniture industry."*

**Bar chart (Turkey economy):**
> *"The two pie charts illustrate how different industry sectors contributed to the economy of Turkey percentage-wise in the years 2000 and 2016."*

## PART 2 — OVERVIEW

> Always start with: **"Overall,"**

**What to write:** 2–4 key general trends. No numbers. No dates. No percentages.

**How to find key features by chart type:**

| Chart type | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Line / bar graph | Which went up? Down? Any fluctuations? Who was highest/lowest at start vs end? Did lines cross? |
| Pie chart | Biggest slice? Smallest slice? What changed most between the two charts? |
| Process diagram | How many stages? Where does it start and end? Any loops? |
| Map / diagram | What was added? Removed? Stayed the same? |

---

### Overview sentence starters by type

**Line / bar (trends):**
> *"Overall, [X] showed an upward trend, while [Y] experienced a downward trend throughout the period. Although [X] initially had a lower rate, it outraced [Y] by the end of the period."*

**Pie chart:**
> *"Overall, at the beginning of the period, [X] contributed the least to the economy while [Y] was the most significant sector. By [end year], [Z] had become the largest segment."*

**Process diagram:**
> *"Overall, the process consists of [N] stages, beginning with [first stage] and ending with [last stage]."*

**Map:**
> *"Overall, [X and Y] disappeared, [Z] was reduced in size, and [new features] were added throughout the period."*

---

### Overview vocabulary — describing size/rank

| Meaning | Phrases to use |
|---|---|
| Biggest | contributed the most · made the highest contribution · made up the biggest part · was the most significant · was the dominant sector |
| Smallest | contributed the least · made the lowest contribution · made up the smallest part · was the least significant |
| Going up | showed an upward trend · experienced a rise · increased steadily · grew throughout the period |
| Going down | showed a downward trend · experienced a decline · fell throughout the period |
| No change | remained constant · stayed the same · was unchanged · held steady |

---

## PART 3 — SPECIFIC DETAILS

Always include:
- Values at the **start AND end** of the period
- Notable **peaks** (highest points) and **troughs** (lowest points)
- **Comparisons** between items
- **Approximate words** when you can't read exact values

---

### How to group your paragraphs (choose ONE method)

| Method | Best for |
|---|---|
| By country / item | bar charts, pie charts with clear categories |
| By trend (rising vs falling) | line graphs — preferred as it follows your overview |
| By time period | tables, complex bar charts |
| Sequential (stage by stage) | process diagrams — always use this |

---

### Expressing percentages — variety of phrases

**Stating a value:**
- accounted for X% · comprised X% · made up X% · stood at X% · was at X% · economic output was X%

**Approximate values (use when the exact number isn't clear from the graph):**
- about · around · approximately · nearly · almost · in the region of · roughly · just over · just under

**Creative fraction phrases (use instead of repeating percentages):**
- almost a quarter (≈25%) · more than a third (>33%) · nearly a half (≈50%)
- around two thirds (≈67%) · more than three quarters (>75%)
- one tenth (10%) · exactly one in ten · approximately a third

> ✗ WRONG: "Sweden's rate was 45% in 2011"
> ✓ RIGHT: "Sweden's rate was **about** 45% in 2011"
> ✓ RIGHT: "the figure reached **almost** 50%" (very close but didn't hit it)
> ✓ RIGHT: "expenditure rose to **just over** $500"

|---|---|---|

### Adverbs — How big / fast was the change?

| Large change | Small change |
|---|---|
| sharply · suddenly · rapidly · dramatically | slightly · gently · gradually · steadily |
| significantly · considerably · markedly · steeply | modestly · marginally · consistently |

| Large | Small |
|---|---|
| sharp · sudden · rapid · dramatic · steep | slight · gentle · gradual · steady |
| significant · considerable · marked · substantial | consistent · modest · marginal |

### Multiplier phrases
- doubled · tripled · quadrupled
- experienced a **threefold** increase
- made a **nearly fourfold** jump
- **more than tripled** over the period

**Change & comparison:** alter · emerge · decline · transition · transformation · exceed · expand · approximate · subsequent · dominant · equivalent · correspond · contrast · parallel

## SPECIAL RULES BY CHART TYPE

### Line graphs & bar charts
- Group data by **trend** (best) or by **country/item**
- Always mention the value at the **start** and **end** of the period
- Note any **crossover points** where lines meet or one overtakes another
- Use "hit a low/high point of" for minimum/maximum values
- Use "remained steady / levelled off" for flat sections

### Pie charts
- Focus on **biggest** and **smallest** slices in each chart
- For two pie charts: compare what **changed** and what **stayed similar**
- Use fraction phrases: "almost a quarter" instead of repeating "24%"
- Use "respectively" for listing two values: "made up 8% and 5% respectively"

### Process diagrams
- Describe stages **sequentially** — never jump around
- Use **"undergo"** when you don't know what happens inside a stage:
  *"the material undergoes refinement in the smelting cabin"*
- Overview = number of stages + beginning + end
- Use time connectors throughout (first of all · then · finally)

### Maps & location diagrams
- Note what was **added**, **removed**, **relocated**, **expanded**, or **stayed the same**
- Useful verbs: was replaced by · was converted into · was demolished · was constructed · remained unchanged · was reduced in size · was extended

### Tables
- Treat like a bar chart — find the biggest, smallest, and most notable values
- Don't list every single number — group by patterns

## COMMON MISTAKES — QUICK REFERENCE

| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right |
|---|---|
| Copying the question card word for word | Paraphrase everything |
| Writing a conclusion | No conclusion in Task 1 — ever |
| Putting numbers/dates in the overview | Overview = general trends only |
| One giant paragraph | Use separate paragraphs per feature |
| Describing every tiny detail | Focus on key trends only |
| "The amount of students" | "The **number** of students" (countable) |
| "In of 2010" / "at 2010" | "**in** 2010" only |
| Exact numbers when graph is unclear | Add "about / around / approximately" |
| Writing "the graph below" | Skip "below" — examiner won't see the chart below your text |
| Changing the graph type name | Never — pie chart stays pie chart |

- [ ] Specific details split into 2–3 paragraphs, grouped logically
- [ ] Approximate words used where values aren't exact (about / nearly / almost)

## BAND 9 MODEL SENTENCES (copy the style, not the words)

**Starting a specific paragraph:**
> *"In 2005, the recycling rates of the UK and Germany were nearly 35% and 20% respectively."*

**Describing a crossover:**
> *"Germany's rate increased sharply throughout the period, exceeding France's rate in 2009 and reaching almost 60% by the end of the period."*

**Describing a fall:**
> *"In early 2005, the recycling rate of France (50%) was the highest among the three countries. However, it dramatically declined to 30% in 2013."*

**Describing a stable period then change:**
> *"During 2007–2008 its annual number of visitors remained constant at about 14 million. However, the figure started to decline rapidly in 2009."*

**Describing a small increase:**
> *"Economic income from trade, utilities and transportation was 14% in 2000 and had a slight growth of 2% in 2016."*

**Describing no change:**
> *"Contribution from the healthcare and education sector remained constant in both years at 17%."*

**Describing a large increase creatively:**
> *"Construction sector accounted for 3% of Turkey's economy in 2000 and experienced a more than threefold increase to one tenth in 2016."*

**Describing a percentage creatively:**
> *"Agriculture, which comprised almost a quarter of Turkey's economy in 2000, fell to 14% in 2016."*
